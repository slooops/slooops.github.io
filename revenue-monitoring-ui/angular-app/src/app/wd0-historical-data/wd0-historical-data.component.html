Significant Attributes from Regression Analysis

Note: Order in ”Booked” status are considered

Based on linear regression analysis, we apply weightages to the main attributes contributing to midclose. We apply different weightages on different WDs to account for variability and dispersion. Our underlying assumption is as we approach WD-0, the variability range will decrease. These weightages are based on historical data.

For product lines predictions, we look at the promise date counts for WD-1 and WD+1 in CG1. For promise date counts on WD-1, we multiply by 0.45 and on counts for WD+1, we multiply by 0.65. Once multiplied, we add the counts.

Steps on WD Weightage Calculations
  • For product predictions on WD-3/-2/-1, we first apply date weightages to promise date counts, sum them up, and then apply WD weightages to the totals. In CG1, we use the same date weightages on promise date counts, sum them, and apply WD weightages to minimum and maximum predictions for accuracy and variability.

For service lines predictions, we look at the flexible invoicing date counts for WD-0 and WD+0 in CG1. For those counts on WD-1, we multiply by 0.65 and on counts for WD+1, we multiply by 1. Once multiplied, we add the counts.

Additionally, for service lines predictions, we look at the future invoice release date counts for WD-1 to WD+2. We multiply the count for WD-1 by 0.47, WD-0 by 0.65, WD+0 by 1, WD+1 by 0.65 and WD+2 by 0.45. Once multiplied, we add the counts.

This date weightage process is the same for all WD-3/-2/-1.

Steps on WD Weightage Calculations

In CG1, we repeat this for flexible invoice and future invoice release counts, summing and applying WD weightages to minimum and maximum predictions.
